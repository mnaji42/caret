The bar's preview comes from the macOS recogniser, not from CrisperWhisper: it has no lexicon, so it writes “use effect” where the inserted text will write useEffect. It answers “is the mic hearing me”, not “will the transcription be right”.

Developers don't speak one language at a time

“Tu as oublié les dependencies dans le useEffect.” Speech models force a single language per segment. In French mode, English technical terms get absorbed phonetically — and those terms are what you dictate all day.

Measured on real mixed-language speech: 32 of 34 technical terms preserved with a lexicon against 29 of 34 without — and no regression on plain sentences.

The mechanism is called vocabulary conditioning: CrisperWhisper 2.0 accepts a list of terms that biases decoding. Your variable names, in-house acronyms and libraries go into settings, and the transcription stops translating them.

You change your mind mid-sentence. The bar keeps up.

Dictation is not a command you fire and endure. Halfway through a sentence you realise the text should not go where it is going. A bar floats at the bottom of the screen while you talk, and everything on it can change without interrupting you.

Mode
Clean text, or word for word. The first removes hesitations and false starts, the second writes exactly what was said — useful for interviews and note-taking.
Destination
The caret of whatever app you are in, or a file. The note file is remembered separately from the current destination, so the round trip costs one click.
Live preview
What is being heard, as you say it. It comes from the macOS recogniser rather than CrisperWhisper, and the interface says so: it answers “is the mic hearing me”, not “will the text be right”.
Collection
Off by default. Once enabled, it archives dictations on your disk so engines can be measured on real speech — the bar says so at all times, and the switch stays within reach. Nothing leaves the machine.

Mode and destination are read when the recording ends, never when it starts. Pressing Notes halfway through a sentence sends that dictation to the file, and the reverse works too. That is what makes the bar useful rather than decorative — and it never takes focus, so the text lands where your caret already is.

Speak now, sort it out later

Not every sentence has a destination at the moment it occurs to you. An idea arrives while you are reading something else, a remark in a meeting, a reminder before it slips away.

Switching to Notes sends the text to a dated Markdown file instead of the caret. You open no app, you choose no location, you do not stop what you were doing. The day accumulates in one file, and you read it back when the time comes.

The file stays a text file, on your disk, readable by any editor. Nothing to export the day you change tools.

Two engines, with very different requirements

One list of requirements would mislead: the gap between the two engines is too wide. The built-in engine is the default and needs no setup at all. CrisperWhisper is the one that writes useEffect, and it is opt-in.

Caspr does not bundle them and never downloads them silently: the licence is shown before any download, and the choice is yours. Numbers, not adjectives

“Blazing fast” means nothing. Here is what was measured, on real voice recordings rather than synthetic speech.

425 ms Full round trip for 13 seconds of speech, model kept warm
4–5× Faster than CrisperWhisper's own Python pipeline, for byte-identical text
94% Of technical terms preserved with a lexicon, against 85% without
0 Bytes sent over the network during a transcription, with either engine

Measured on a MacBook Pro M4 Pro, 48 GB, macOS 26.6. Two things explain the gap with the reference pipeline: it costs roughly 1.5 seconds of overhead per transcription, and Whisper always encodes 30 seconds even when you spoke for 5 — shrinking that window to 15 seconds removes nearly half the encoder time without changing a letter of the output. Below 15 seconds the model leaves its training distribution, and Caspr does not go there. The scripts that reproduce these numbers are in the repository.

Nothing to send, so nothing to protect

Privacy is not a promise Caspr asks you to take on trust, it is a consequence of how it is built: there is no server to send anything to. Transcription happens in your Mac's memory, with either engine.

The code is public. You can read what the app does, build it yourself, or watch for yourself that no request goes out.
