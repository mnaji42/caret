You don't speak the way you write

We hesitate, we backtrack, we start the sentence again. Ordinary dictation writes all of it down, every “er” included, and then you reread it to clean it up. Caspr writes what you meant.

Ordinary dictation

“So er hang on, we'll we'll push the meeting to Thursday no wait Friday morning er and let Sarah know”

You reread it, you cut, you punctuate. The time dictation saved goes straight back into fixing it.

With Caspr

“We'll push the meeting to Friday morning, and let Sarah know.”

Hesitations, restarts and false starts are removed; punctuation and capitals are inferred from the meaning.

The “clean text” mode comes from CrisperWhisper, and it is the default. “Word for word” does the opposite and writes exactly what was said, hesitations included — useful for interviews. The macOS engines return a single text, with no cleanup pass.

And the words other engines mangle

Plenty of trades borrow their vocabulary from another language. A French trader says “le spread s'est écarté sur le forward”, a researcher “le peer review a retoqué le protocole”, a developer “les dependencies dans le useEffect”. Speech models force one language per segment, so in French mode those words get absorbed phonetically.

How three technical terms are transcribed by Whisper-family models in French mode and by Caspr
What you say Speech recognition, French mode Caspr, with a lexicon
useEffect use effect useEffect
dependencies dépendances dependencies
React react React

Measured on real speech mixing French and English: 32 of 34 terms preserved with a lexicon against 29 of 34 without — and no regression on plain sentences.

The mechanism is called vocabulary conditioning: the engine accepts a list of terms that biases decoding. Yours goes into settings — client names, molecules, tickers, in-house jargon — and the transcription stops translating them. The three examples above come from the test corpus; the mechanism itself knows no particular field.

You change your mind mid-sentence. The bar keeps up.

Dictation is not a command you fire and endure. Halfway through a sentence you realise the text should not go where it is going. A bar floats at the bottom of the screen while you talk, and everything on it can change without interrupting you.

Mode
Clean text, or word for word. The first removes hesitations and false starts, the second writes exactly what was said.
Destination
The caret of whatever app you are in, or a file. The note file is remembered separately: the round trip costs one click.
Live preview
What is being heard, as you say it. It answers “is the mic hearing me”, not “will the text be right”.
Collection
Off by default. Once enabled it archives dictations on your disk so engines can be measured — and nothing leaves the machine.

Mode and destination are read when the recording ends, never when it starts. Pressing Notes halfway through a sentence sends that dictation to the file, and the reverse works too. That is what makes the bar useful rather than decorative — and it never takes focus, so the text lands where your caret already is.

Speak now, sort it out later

Not every sentence has a destination the moment it occurs to you. Switch to Notes and the text goes to a dated Markdown file instead of the caret — no app to open, nothing to leave behind. The day accumulates, and you read it back when the time comes.

~/Notes/2026-08-20.md
  1. 09:14Remind Sarah the file is waiting on her review.
  2. 09:41Churn is up on the March cohort, dig in before Friday.
  3. 11:02Idea: revisit the protocol after the threshold change.
  4. 14:27Check the spread on the long maturities.

The file stays a text file, on your disk, readable by any editor. Nothing to export the day you change tools.

The choice comes down to one question

macOS, or CrisperWhisper. The first is already there and asks for nothing. The second knows your lexicon, but it has requirements.

On the macOS side there are in fact two versions, and Caspr picks the best one your machine can run without asking you: system Dictation, present since macOS 10.15 and all the way down to Intel Macs, and Apple Intelligence, introduced with macOS 26 and finer on long passages. Availability is measured on the machine, never inferred from a version number.

CrisperWhisper 2.0's weights fall under a Nyra Health non-commercial research licence. Caspr does not bundle them and never downloads them silently: the licence is shown before any download, and the choice is yours. Read the licence.

What was measured

On real voice recordings rather than synthetic speech. The scripts that reproduce these numbers are in the repository.

425 ms Full round trip for 13 seconds of speech, model kept warm
4–5× Faster than the reference Python pipeline, for byte-identical text
94% Of technical terms preserved with a lexicon, against 85% without
0 Bytes sent over the network during a transcription, with any engine

Measured on a MacBook Pro M4 Pro, 48 GB, macOS 26.6. Two things explain the gap with the reference pipeline: it costs roughly 1.5 seconds of overhead per transcription, and Whisper always encodes 30 seconds even when you spoke for 5 — shrinking that window to 15 seconds removes nearly half the encoder time without changing a letter of the output. Below 15 seconds the model leaves its training distribution, and Caspr does not go there.

Local by construction, open source on principle

Privacy is not a promise Caspr asks you to take on trust, it is a consequence of how it is built: there is no server to send anything to. And you don't have to take my word for it — the whole codebase is public, MIT licensed, yours to read and to build.

  • Free and open source. The Swift app and the Python engine are MIT licensed, on GitHub. Nothing held back, no subscription, no account.
  • No telemetry. No usage statistics, no tracker, no silent crash reports.
  • Offline for real. Turn off Wi-Fi and dictation behaves identically.
  • One optional request. The update check queries GitHub, and switches off in settings.
  • An uninstaller that uninstalls. The Python environment, the weights, the caches and the preferences go with it — and only what is actually there.
